    This does not mean that Delhivery has a logistics problem

    Only people whose problem was not fixed through your normal support end up commenting here. Most parcels arrive fine, and most support tickets get solved.

    But how would a potential client feel seeing this?

    Why this isn't a logistics problem

    Shipping has a physical ceiling: incomplete addresses, people not at home, cash-on-delivery refusals, weather. Your industry already invests heavily here. We are not proposing to fix the last mile.

    8–20%of parcels worldwide fail the first delivery attempt
    97.2%first-attempt success in the US, among the best anywhere
    ~26%of cash-on-delivery orders in India come back, against under 2% prepaid

    This also isn't a Delhivery problem

    We checked whether this is only you. It isn't. Wherever an online business depends on real-world delivery, problems that don't get fixed privately end up in public.

    • XpressbeesSame pattern under unrelated posts.
    • ShadowfaxSame pattern.
    • AmazonOn its main LinkedIn page too, though less visible.

    Because everyone has it, the first carrier to break the pattern stands out.

    It's a recovery problem.

    Double deviation means two things go wrong in a row. First, the parcel fails. Second, the attempt to fix it fails too. The first one is a logistics problem, but the second one is what makes people angry enough to post on social media.

    3–20%fail the first delivery.

    50–80%feel the complaint ended without resolution.

    32%of complainants post about it on social media.

    It's all loops.

    Current fixes create vicious cycles, and ex-customers who actively hurt the brand image. We've mapped out the system's behaviour in the diagram below.

    Two of today's fixes show the pattern most clearly.

    Fix 1: Responding to comments

    Success to the Successful

    Every public reply pulls support effort toward social media. Public complaints get solved faster, so more people complain in public, so even more effort moves there. Tickets get less effort, get solved worse, and fewer people trust them.

    Fix 2: Blocking comments

    Fixes that Fail

    Blocking comments makes complaints disappear from your posts, for now. The angry customers didn't go away. After a delay, they started writing full posts on their own feeds, where all their connections can see them and you can't moderate them.

    The proof

    With comments turned off, complaints like this now appear as full posts on customers' own feeds. Delhivery Support has to reply there, in front of the customer's whole network.

    What we'd do for you

    Start with research and measurement, so every recommendation rests on your numbers, not ours.

    1. Research and measure the recovery funnel

      On-ground research with your support and operations teams and at your hubs, alongside your data: how many customers contact support after a failure, through which channels, how many get resolved, how long it takes, and how many end up public.

    2. Find where it leaks

      Walk the funnel with your teams, and with customers who complained, to see which stages break and why.

    3. Prototype the fixes

      Design and test changes for the two or three biggest leaks, from who owns a case to settling disputed delivery status.
